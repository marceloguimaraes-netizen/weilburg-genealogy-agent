# Brazil — sources beyond FamilySearch for German immigrants

FamilySearch has many indexes and microfilms of Brazilian parish and civil records, but its
indexes have transcription errors and gaps. The aim here is to (a) verify those indexes
against originals and (b) find records FamilySearch does not have — above all, the record
that names the **German village of origin**.

Entries marked *(verify)* are leads that still need checking.

## Which records exist, and when

| Period | Births | Marriages | Deaths |
|---|---|---|---|
| before 1889 | Parish baptism (Catholic, or Lutheran congregation) | Parish marriage | Parish burial |
| from 1 Jan 1889 | **Civil registry** (Decreto 9.886 of 1888, in force 1889) + baptism | Civil marriage optional for Catholics at first | Civil registry |
| from 24 Jan 1890 | Civil registry | **Civil marriage mandatory** (Decreto 181) | Civil registry |

Civil registration was adopted slowly, especially in rural colonies — for the 1890s–1910s
always look for the church record as well. Late registrations (*registro tardio*) of a birth,
made decades afterwards, are common and are secondary evidence.

## Finding the German village (the key problem)

Brazilian records often say only *natural da Alemanha*, *da Prússia* or *alemão*. Records that
are more likely to name the village or the Duchy of Nassau:
1. **Marriage records** — church and civil — often give the bride's and groom's birthplaces and parents.
2. **Lutheran congregation books** — German-speaking pastors often wrote the exact village.
3. **Catholic baptisms** in Brazil often name **both sets of grandparents** (*avós paternos / maternos*), which links three generations at once.
4. **Death records and inventories** (*inventários*, probate) — list heirs and sometimes origin.
5. **Naturalisation files** and colony registers (*livros de matrícula de colonos*).
6. **Gravestones** and German-language obituaries.
7. On the German side: the HHStAW emigration file (*Auswanderungsakte*), which names the destination.

Then confirm in the Weilburg-area church book: the person's baptism, their siblings, and their parents' marriage.

## National archives and databases

- **Arquivo Nacional — SIAN** (sian.an.gov.br, free registration): arrival lists for Rio de
  Janeiro from 1875 and Santos from 1891, Ilha das Flores hostel from 1883, **naturalisation
  files 1823–1959**, foreigners' registration (SPMAF, 1939–1988). For ship lists you usually
  need the ship's name and approximate date. Date ranges vary by source — check the SIAN catalogue.
- **Museu da Imigração do Estado de São Paulo** (museudaimigracao.org.br) — free digital
  collection: Hospedaria do Brás registration books 1882–1958 (searchable by full name), ship
  lists for Santos from 1888. Originals at the **Arquivo Público do Estado de São Paulo (APESP)**.
  The museum's research centre (CPPR) does searches on request.
- **Arquivo Público do Estado do Espírito Santo — Projeto Imigrantes**
  (ape.es.gov.br/imigrantes): database of more than 50,000 immigrants arriving from 1812 on,
  including Germans, with age, occupation, religion and region of origin. ES had German colonies
  (Santa Isabel 1847, Santa Leopoldina, Domingos Martins) with many settlers from the Hunsrück and Hesse *(verify)*.
- **Hemeroteca Digital Brasileira** (bndigital.bn.gov.br/hemeroteca-digital) — digitised
  newspapers, including German-language papers such as the *Kolonie-Zeitung* (Joinville) and
  *Deutsche Zeitung* (Porto Alegre) *(verify titles and years)*: arrivals, obituaries, legal notices.

## Church records

- **Lutheran:** the IECLB (Igreja Evangélica de Confissão Luterana no Brasil) congregations keep
  their own books; some are filmed by FamilySearch. Ask the local congregation or the synod.
  There is also an IECLB historical archive — confirm on IECLB's site before writing.
  Some congregations belong to the IELB (Missouri Synod) instead.
- **Catholic:** the archive of the diocese (*Cúria Diocesana / Arquivo da Cúria Metropolitana*) of
  the parish at that time; parishes were often split, so the older parish may hold the records.

## Civil and legal records

- **Cartório de Registro Civil das Pessoas Naturais** of the municipality (or *distrito*): ask for a
  *certidão de inteiro teor* (full transcript), not a summary *certidão* — the full transcript keeps
  witnesses, parents, grandparents and annotations. Many registries can be reached through the
  national portal of the civil registries (Registro Civil / ARPEN) *(verify current portal)*.
- **Tabelionato** (notary) — deeds, powers of attorney, wills.
- **Inventários e testamentos** — State court archives (Tribunal de Justiça) or state archives (APERS in RS).

## Name forms

Given names were often translated, and surnames respelled by clerks:

| German | Portuguese |
|---|---|
| Johann / Johannes | João |
| Wilhelm | Guilherme |
| Heinrich | Henrique |
| Philipp | Felipe / Filipe |
| Georg | Jorge |
| Friedrich | Frederico |
| Karl / Carl | Carlos |
| Peter | Pedro |
| Jakob | Jacó / Jacob |
| Katharina | Catarina |
| Margaretha | Margarida |
| Elisabeth | Isabel |
| Maria Magdalena | Maria Madalena |

Surnames: Schmidt → Smith / Schmitt, Müller → Muller / Miller, Weber → Veber, Schneider → Snaider,
Becker → Beker, dropped umlauts and doubled consonants. Women may appear under
their maiden name, their husband's name, or both.

## Reading Portuguese records

*batizado* (baptised), *nascido aos …* (born on …), *filho legítimo / natural de* (legitimate /
natural son of), *padrinhos* (godparents), *avós paternos / maternos* (paternal / maternal grandparents),
*recebidos em matrimônio* (married), *viúvo/viúva de* (widower/widow of), *faleceu* / *sepultado*
(died / buried), *natural de* (born in), *residente em* (living in), *lavrador* (farmer), *colono* (colonist).
